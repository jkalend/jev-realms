# Visual Target Research — Laya Realms

Goal: pick a visual target for the Bevy migration that reads "premium depth" (Diablo II was the starting reference; the search was broadened to "Tyranny, Grim Dawn and so on", and later to Darkest Dungeon I/II for thematic ideas).

Current state for calibration (from `docs/VISUAL_ROADMAP.md`): procedural 32-unit sprite tiles and authored actor silhouettes in macroquad, amber-on-charcoal palette, zoomable 16–64 px tile view, presentation strictly separated from sim. Single dev, RTX 4090 workstation, Bevy target. Bevy feature gating is covered by the sibling research in this directory; this doc only names *which* rendering features each candidate recipe needs.

Source convention: official dev posts, GDC Vault / Unreal Fest talks, engine-maker spotlights first; press/interviews second. All links verified reachable during research (2026-09).

## 1. Reference games

### 1.1 Grim Dawn (Crate Entertainment, 2016)

| Aspect | Findings |
|---|---|
| Camera | Full 3D world, perspective camera at near-isometric pitch (~45–55°), player-controllable rotation and zoom, inherited from the licensed Titan Quest engine ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grim_Dawn); engine history: [Crate FAQ — Technical stuff](https://forums.crateentertainment.com/t/grim-dawn-faq-compilation-of-dev-feedback/99268/7)). |
| Art pipeline | Full 3D, hand-built: terrain sculpted/painted with texture-blend brushes on a mesh ([brush/tool tutorial by Crate](https://forums.crateentertainment.com/t/tutorial-brush-tool-and-texture-blending/33699)), modular kit props, characters as 3D skinned meshes. Environment art was the *first* thing production started because level design consumed it ([Grim Misadventure #10](https://forums.crateentertainment.com/t/grim-misadventures-10-the-prizes-we-seek/27981)); environment-art leadership hired in from Irrational/BioShock ([Crate announcement](https://forums.crateentertainment.com/t/ex-irrational-environment-director-joins-crate/29192)). Art is tuned for readability *at play distance*, not concept-art beauty ([Grim Misadventure #41](https://forums.crateentertainment.com/t/grim-misadventure-41-from-concept-to-art/28509)). |
| Palette + lighting | Deliberate "moodier", "gritty" desaturation vs Titan Quest's "sunny, super-saturated world" — a stated art-direction choice, with post-process saturation/contrast controls ([Screenshots Posted!, p.8](https://forums.crateentertainment.com/t/screenshots-posted/26938?page=8)). Not monochrome: each biome gets a restrained but distinct palette identity (brown/green slums, red-orange autumn forest, golden farmland) ([Abandoned Waterfront](https://forums.crateentertainment.com/t/abandoned-waterfront-screenshots/27522)). Weather "climates", fog, and a dedicated character light rig keep the player readable against dark scenes ([Dark & Stormy](https://forums.crateentertainment.com/t/dark-stormy/27038?page=2)); bloom tuned as a stylistic lever, not a blanket effect ([New Environment WIP](https://forums.crateentertainment.com/t/new-enviroment-wip-update/27045/31)). Saturation is *spent on gameplay*: Aetherial green, Chthonic red, fire, magic FX against a low-value backdrop. |
| Why it reads expensive | (1) Dense hand-authored clutter/set dressing per area ("sense of place" pass). (2) Atmospheric layering — weather, fog, regional grading — on every scene. (3) Spell/skill FX with colored lighting and particles carrying all the saturation budget. |
| Indie feasibility | **Harsh:** the look is 90% content volume (years of kit + env passes by a dedicated team) — do not chase the density. Liftable cheaply: the *palette discipline* (desaturated world + saturated gameplay accents), the character-light-rig trick (readability light independent of scene light), and per-biome color grading. A low-poly 3D Grim-lite is feasible; present-day Grim Dawn is not. |

### 1.2 Tyranny (Obsidian, 2016)

| Aspect | Findings |
|---|---|
| Camera | Fixed "isometric" illusion: an orthographic camera in Unity at the exact angle used for the offline render — the trick only holds at that angle ([PoE Update #79, Graphics and Rendering](https://eternity.obsidian.net/eternity/news/update--79-graphics-and-rendering-)). Same tech as Pillars of Eternity, moved to Unity 5 ([GameBanshee interview](https://www.gamebanshee.com/interviews/117654-tyranny-interview.html)). |
| Art pipeline | **Pre-rendered 2D backgrounds + real-time 3D characters.** Environments built as detailed 3D scenes in Maya, rendered to multiple passes — final, depth, normal, albedo — then touched up by hand by concept artists, i.e. the Infinity-Engine look rebuilt with modern passes ([Update #34](https://eternity.obsidian.net/eternity/news/update-34-first-art-update), [Update #30](https://eternity.obsidian.net/eternity/news/update-30-how-stuff-is-made), [Update #79](https://eternity.obsidian.net/eternity/news/update--79-graphics-and-rendering-)). Characters/props/FX are live 3D skinned meshes; the depth pass gives per-pixel occlusion so characters walk *behind* painted scenery; a key/fill light rig plus a shadow-control map matches character lighting to the baked background ([Update #79](https://eternity.obsidian.net/eternity/news/update--79-graphics-and-rendering-)). Tyranny restyled the same pipeline toward simplified, sharply defined, more surreal forms to avoid "Pillars 1.5" ([Obsidian forum](https://forums.obsidian.net/topic/93628-obsidian-entertainment-studio-tour/page/3/)). |
| Palette + lighting | Grounded muted fantasy in PoE; Tyranny goes more stylized and cold — war-torn desaturated environments with strong atmospheric depth (foreground/mid/background value separation baked into the render), characters lit by a matched directional + ambient sampled from the background. |
| Why it reads expensive | (1) Backgrounds are literally offline-rendered V-ray-quality images — fidelity ceiling of a render farm, at 2D cost. (2) The lighting-match rig makes 3D characters blend into the painting instead of floating on it. (3) Concept-artist paintover pass on every scene. |
| Indie feasibility | The *multi-pass* version (depth/normal/albedo from a 3D scene, per-pixel occlusion) is a serious tools investment — Obsidian had tools engineers. A single dev *can* do a degraded version (Blender-rendered backgrounds + depth pass + lit 3D/2D actors), but every hand-crafted background fights procedural map generation: a roguelike that regenerates maps cannot pre-paint its world. The reusable idea is the **depth-passed painted plane + matched character light rig**, not the content pipeline. |

### 1.3 Hades (Supergiant, 2020)

| Aspect | Findings |
|---|---|
| Camera | Fixed isometric 2D / 2.5D; single composed view per chamber, no rotation. |
| Art pipeline | Environments: hand-painted 2D isometric tilesets taken from graybox layout through painting, integration, polish ([Supergiant environment-artist listing describing the pipeline](https://www.supergiantgames.com/blog/10/)); environment art by Joanne Tran under art director Jen Zee ([Supergiant team page](https://www.supergiantgames.com/team/)). Characters: NPC/creature art starts as hand-painted 2D, is then **sculpted in ZBrush against the 2D portrait**, retopo'd in Maya, textured in Substance with baked AO/curvature/thickness maps, and finished with a **hand-painted black line-art layer** to preserve the illustrated look ([GameDeveloper breakdown of the Nyx character workflow](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/art/learn-how-supergiant-brought-i-hades-i-hand-painted-characters-to-life); [Supergiant's own video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cYJ6d1ifSqA)). Process context: [GDC fireside chat with Amir Rao](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1027189/LIVE-Fireside-Chat-Amir-Rao); [Jen Zee on Hades II art](https://gameinformer.com/exclusive-video/2024/07/05/supergiant-games-art-director-jen-zee-on-the-making-of-hades-ii). |
| Palette + lighting | Saturated, high-value contrast, comic-art chromatic palette — the anti-Grim-Dawn: bright character and FX colors on clean mid-tone grounds; strong rim lighting and glow on attacks; bold graphic shapes with painted black line art so everything reads at speed. |
| Why it reads expensive | (1) A top-tier illustrator's hand on literally every surface (one art director = absolute consistency). (2) Painterly characters that move — the ZBrush→overpaint hybrid gives 3D lighting on an illustrated surface. (3) Extreme animation/VFX polish and palette discipline per god/biome (theme-color sets). |
| Indie feasibility | **Art-talent-bound, not tech-bound.** Nothing here is hard to *render*; everything is hard to *paint*. With a mid-tier painter's output this collapses to "D2-looking indie #5000". Liftable at zero art cost: god/biome **theme-color discipline** and the baked-AO-then-line-art texturing shortcut if we ever use 3D characters. |

### 1.4 Path of Exile 1 / 2 (Grinding Gear Games, 2013 / 2024)

| Aspect | Findings |
|---|---|
| Camera | Full 3D, fixed perspective camera at isometric-like pitch, no rotation — the renderer is deliberately specialized for a single view direction ([ExileCon 2023: Rendering Path of Exile 2 — Alexander Sannikov, GGG](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TrHHTQqmAaM)). |
| Art pipeline | Full 3D on GGG's proprietary in-house engine; PoE2 upgraded it to PBR materials/lighting, screen-space techniques, GPU particles, and experiments in real-time GI (Radiance Cascades) ([ExileCon 2023 talk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TrHHTQqmAaM)); the PBR transition was motivated publicly in 2019 ([ExileCon 2019 "Evolving Path of Exile's Renderer"](https://webcdn.pathofexile.com/forum/view-thread/3402531/filter-account-type/staff); [GGG bloom/PBR announcement](https://www.pathofexile.com/forum/view-thread/2907745)). PoE1 itself showed how far a small team goes incrementally: point-light shadows and GI approximations were added years after launch ([GGG GI/shadows preview](https://www.pathofexile.com/forum/view-thread/2198905)). |
| Palette + lighting | Grimdark desaturated base like Grim Dawn but colder/danker; lighting does the premium work — colored point lights on every torch/brazier/skill, strong shadow contrast; FX readability became a problem at scale (skill FX overwhelming the screen is a famous PoE pain point) — the Sannikov talks frame rendering around keeping thousands of lights/particles legible. |
| Why it reads expensive | (1) Lighting richness per scene (many colored shadow-casting lights). (2) Monetized polish: the cash-shop MTX FX effectively fund and showcase the renderer. (3) PoE2's animation/effects density per encounter. |
| Indie feasibility | PoE1's **2013-era look is the honest ceiling** for a solo 3D attempt — and it was already called dated in 2013. The transferable lessons are architectural, not content: fixed camera lets the renderer be deeply specialized (Bevy equivalent: one projection, everything tuned to it); colored point lights + shadows at tile-critical positions are affordable on an RTX 4090 even in Bevy today. Do not copy the FX density. |

### 1.5 Disco Elysium (ZA/UM, 2019)

| Aspect | Findings |
|---|---|
| Camera | Fixed isometric-style view with a deliberately restrained camera; the team treats each scene as one composed painting — "you can design the entire game as if it was a painting" ([Rostov, PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/rpg/why-does-isometric-perspective-suit-disco-elysium-you-can-design-the-entire-game-as-if-it-was-a-painting/)). Built in Unity. |
| Art pipeline | Real hybrid: concept sketch → 3D block-in (Blender/Maya) → multiple render passes (diffuse, shadow, **height**, **normal**, object-ID "clown" maps, hand-authored **light-field** maps) → Photoshop painterly paintover → reprojected onto the block-in geometry via camera-projected UVs so the painting aligns with real geometry for collision, occlusion and dynamic lights ([ZA/UM devblog: Tiling of the World](https://discoelysium.com/devblog/2016/08/18/tiling-of-the-world), [Gorgeous Ground](https://discoelysium.com/devblog/2016/03/29/gorgeous-ground)). The look was literally built as "a cover band for Pillars of Eternity" that evolved its own identity ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/rpg/disco-elysium-devs-built-the-rpgs-visual-style-as-a-cover-band-for-obsidians-great-comeback-game-pillars-of-eternity/)). Pipeline documented in GDC talk by technical artist Siim Raidma, "Turpentine Fumes and Shaders: Art Tech in Disco Elysium" ([GDC 2020 coverage](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/art/come-to-gdc-and-see-how-i-disco-elysium-i-s-unique-style-was-achieved-)). |
| Palette + lighting | Oil-painting identity: broken color, visible brushwork, atmospheric depth (fog/depth-of-field in the distance), muted cold base with warm accent pools; dynamic colored lights and moving elements (trees, flags) over the painted base so it doesn't feel static. |
| Why it reads expensive | (1) It is literally gallery-grade painting carried into real-time. (2) The multi-pass geometry backing means dynamic light/shadow still behaves correctly over "flat" art. (3) Consistent post stack — fog, DoF, grain — making every frame a composed image. |
| Indie feasibility | Same anti-fit with procedural generation as Tyranny, squared: **four years of pipeline by a classically trained painter plus a technical artist**. The transferable piece is the *idea* of a normal/height pass under painted 2D art so lights react to brush strokes — which is exactly what normal-mapped sprites give a pixel game for free. |

### 1.6 Octopath Traveler (Acquire / Square Enix, 2018) — HD-2D

| Aspect | Findings |
|---|---|
| Camera | Perspective 3D camera with deliberately **narrowed FOV / softened perspective** to keep a 2D-RPG feel; fixed-ish elevated angles with tilt-shift-lens-like staging ([Business 4Gamer report on the Unreal Fest East 2018 technical talk](https://business.4gamer.net/article/1810/18101801/)). |
| Art pipeline | UE4. 16-bit-style pixel sprites animated as **Paper Flipbooks** (Spine-style skeletal sprites were rejected as looking "PS1-era", not SNES-era), hand-corrected at the pixel level — one character/job ≈ 365 sprites, protagonists ≈ 35,000 sprites total ([Business 4Gamer](https://business.4gamer.net/article/1810/18101801/)). Worlds are real 3D "dioramas" with strong vertical layering (flat maps looked empty from the camera), pixel-textured ground under **normal maps, specular, dynamic lights and shadows**; combat FX carry point lights so effects light the environment ([Business 4Gamer](https://business.4gamer.net/article/1810/18101801/); [Epic's developer interview](https://www.unrealengine.com/spotlights/octopath-traveler-s-hd-2d-art-style-and-story-make-for-a-jrpg-dream-come-true); [Unreal Fest Europe 2019 talk page](https://www.unrealengine.com/events/unreal-fest-europe-2019/the-fusion-of-nostalgia-and-novelty-in-the-development-of-octopath-traveler)). |
| Palette + lighting | Post-process is the identity: **Bokeh depth of field, bloom, color grading, ambient cubemap, vignette**, tightly budgeted lens flares, adjusted per map ([Business 4Gamer](https://business.4gamer.net/article/1810/18101801/)). Early prototypes with crisp sprites and deep focus read as merely "old"; the remembered-SNES softness came from DoF/bloom/grade unifying pixels with 3D light ([E3 2018 interview, 4Gamer](https://www.4gamer.net/games/368/G036845/20180615177/); [Siliconera/Famitsu summary](https://www.siliconera.com/project-octopath-traveler-developers-answer-project-started-troubles-developing-hd-2d/)). |
| Why it reads expensive | (1) The "miniature diorama" photographic frame (tilt-shift DoF + staged verticality) — a cinematographic, not gamey, read. (2) 2D textures participate in a real 3D lighting model (pixel water reflects sunsets). (3) Massive sprite volume with hand correction — 35k protagonist sprites is pure content spend. |
| Indie feasibility | The **post stack is 80% of the magic and is the cheap 80%** — bloom/DoF/grading are engine features (Bevy: bloom since 0.9, extended in 0.16; DoF built-in — see sibling Bevy research; the PoE2-talk-inspired decals and atmospheric scattering landed in Bevy 0.16: [bevy.org/news/bevy-0-16](https://bevy.org/news/bevy-0-16/)). The killer cost is sprite volume at Octopath's frame counts (dozens of directions × actions × characters). Solo answer: keep frame counts near-CT-trigger low (idle/walk/attack/hit, 1–2 facings), exactly what a turn-based tactical game already tolerates. |

### 1.7 Songs of Conquest (Lavapotion, 2024) — the closest precedent

| Aspect | Findings |
|---|---|
| Camera | 3D camera with zoom and rotation over a diorama world; readability preserved because gameplay-critical art is billboarded to the camera ([80 Level interview](https://80.lv/articles/mixing-2d-billboards-and-3d-environments-in-a-game)). |
| Art pipeline | **2D pixel-art billboards embedded in a procedural 3D lighting/camera system**, Unity + custom "Cartography" rendering: terrain/cliffs/coast generated as semi-procedural 3D chunks (marching squares); trees/buildings/units are shader-billboarded 2D sprites with GPU atlas animation and reconstructed normals so flat art receives scene lighting; characters/buildings deliberately did *not* get the expensive billboard treatment (cost/benefit call) ([80 Level: Mixing 2D Billboards and 3D Environments](https://80.lv/articles/mixing-2d-billboards-and-3d-environments-in-a-game); [80 Level: The Shader Approach to Billboarding](https://80.lv/articles/the-shader-approach-to-billboarding)). Lighting is a **top-down projected real-time lightmap** rendered by a separate camera from "lighting sprites" — carrying colored lights, cloud shadows, day/night — applied across terrain and billboards alike ([80 Level interview](https://80.lv/articles/mixing-2d-billboards-and-3d-environments-in-a-game)). Started as purist pixel art, pivoted to 2.5D deliberately ([Explorminate Q&A](https://explorminate.org/songs-of-conquest-qa/)). Built by a small team (c. 3–5 core for most of development: [Lavapotion press kit](https://www.lavapotion.com/press)). |
| Palette + lighting | Warm storybook pixel palette uplifted by the lighting layer: soft colored light pools, cloud shadows, day/night tinting, bloom, vignette, grading, DoF. Pixel constraint below, photographic atmosphere above — the same contrast trick as Octopath, but on a procedurally generated map. |
| Why it reads expensive | (1) Light wraps the whole scene coherently (sprites, terrain, shadows from one projected source). (2) Atmospheric breadth — cloud shadows and day/night sweep a map that is "just pixels". (3) Diorama staging + post stack, same lesson as Octopath. |
| Indie feasibility | **This is the reference to copy.** Small team, Unity, procedural maps, pixel art, custom-but-scoped rendering (one billboard shader + one projected-lightmap system). Scope cuts they made (no billboard complexity for characters) are exactly the cuts a solo dev makes. In Bevy the two systems map to: projected light texture over the map (render-to-texture) + normal-lit sprites (see sibling Bevy research on normal-mapped sprites / 2D lighting options). |

### 1.8 Darkest Dungeon (Red Hook, 2016)

| Aspect | Findings |
|---|---|
| Camera | 2D side-scrolling, orthographic, composed "tableau" scenes (combat line-ups, corridor walks). Not tile-isometric at all — included here for art identity and thematics, not camera. |
| Art pipeline | Hand-drawn 2D assets in a hand-inked gothic comic style (influences: Mignola, Guy Davis, medieval woodcut/illuminated manuscript), assembled from separately drawn parts and posed/animated in-engine as paper-doll rigs — deliberately low animation cost chosen as a production strategy ([Bourassa interview, Gamespresso](https://www.gamespresso.com/2015/06/interview-chris-bourassa-darkest-dungeon/); [GDC 2016: "A Torch in the Dark" — classic-style postmortem talk](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1023082/A-Torch-in-the-Dark), [slides PDF](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2016/Presentations/Bourassa_Chris_a%20torch%20in.pdf)). |
| Palette + lighting | Hard black outlines, "pooling blacks", parchment/grayscale base, **restrained crimson accents**; the torch is the light source: warm amber pool against blue-black gloom; flame state drives scene contrast ([Bourassa interview](https://www.gamespresso.com/2015/06/interview-chris-bourassa-darkest-dungeon/); [GDC 2016 talk](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1023082/A-Torch-in-the-Dark)). |
| Why it reads expensive | (1) One uncompromising graphic language (ink + black pools + red) applied to every asset, UI included. (2) Illustration quality per pose. (3) Effects economy: flashes, screen shakes, speed lines sell impact instead of simulation. |
| Indie feasibility | **Art-talent-bound like Hades but cheaper per asset** (fewer states, in-engine posing = the original indie-scope hack). The graphic-language discipline (black/red/parchment applied uniformly to sprites, UI, and FX) is skill-limited but content-cheap, and it suits a turn-based game's static tableau moments. |
| THEMATIC IDEAS | **Torch as mechanic**: DD1's torch level literally dims the world, raises crit/loot risk-reward — Laya Realms already has torch light-radius + night cycles → **STEAL**: bind scene exposure/saturation to light radius so a dying torch visibly decays the frame (cheap: one global grade uniform). **Affliction/quirk flavor**: stress state gets a visual tell (pose, tint, shake) → **STEAL**: morale/stress states as cheap sprite overlays + screen tint, not new rigs. **Black-outline/red-accent language**: → **STEAL** for UI framing and damage/affliction FX — harmonizes with the existing amber-on-charcoal palette (swap parchment→amber and it is already Laya-flavored). **Narrator-style UI voice**: deadpan one-line commentary on events → **THEMATIC ONLY** for text cadence (writing cost only); VO is out of scope. **Paper-doll silhouette poses**: → **STEAL the principle, not the style**: Laya already has authored silhouettes — add strong *pose* states for stress/melee/death rather than more animation frames. |

### 1.9 Darkest Dungeon II (Red Hook, 2022)

| Aspect | Findings |
|---|---|
| Camera | 3D with cinematic, staged cameras: fixed side-ish combat tableaus with reframing per action, plus 3D travel dioramas; composition is directed, not free ([GDC 2024: Critical! Combat Presentation Tools for DD2 — animation sequencing/previs for a small team](https://gdcvault.com/play/1034466/Independent-Games-Summit-Critical-Combat)). |
| Art pipeline | **Illustration-first 3D**: environment assets are drawn in 2D, then 3D geometry is built to *support the drawing*, preserving linework and painted detail; rigged 3D characters chosen specifically for richer animation/FX/lighting and more mature proportions, with the constraint that tech must "enhance or evolve" the 2D style, not overwhelm it ([GameDeveloper: road-trip-from-hell interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-a-theme-of-a-road-trip-from-hell-would-shape-darkest-dungeon-ii); [PC Gamer interview](https://www.pcgamer.com/interview-darkest-dungeon-2s-new-3d-look-monsters-the-narrator-and-its-surprising-aspirational-spirit/); [Epic Games Store interview](https://store.epicgames.com/no/news/darkest-dungeon-2-early-access-interview); [Red Hook about page](https://www.darkestdungeon.com/darkest-dungeon-2/about/)). The failed-experiments story of translating the 2D legacy into 3D is documented in [GDC 2024: Facing Our Failures — 3D Environments from a 2D Legacy](https://gdcvault.com/play/1034192/Facing-Our-Failures-Creating-3D). Result described at preview as a "pop-up book diorama" ([Save or Quit preview](https://saveorquit.com/2021/11/04/preview-darkest-dungeon-2/)). |
| Palette + lighting | DD1's ink/black/red language carried into volumetric light: theatrical spot-lighting on characters, heavy fog layers separating foreground/mid/background, smoke and ember particles; lighting is staged per tableau, not neutral. |
| Why it reads expensive | (1) Proof that a 2D paper style can be rebuilt in 3D *without losing identity* — the geometry serves the drawing. (2) Cinematic animation beats (camera reframe + pose + VFX per skill). (3) Theatrical fog/depth staging makes small 3D scenes feel enormous. |
| Indie feasibility | The specific pipeline (2D draw → 3D match → toon-heavy shading per hero) was built by a ~25-person studio over years, including documented failures — **not repeatable solo**. The reusable principles are free: illustration-first ("build 3D around the art"), fog-sliced depth planes, and staged lighting per encounter. |
| THEMATIC IDEAS | **A 2D identity can survive a 3D rebuild if geometry serves the drawing** → **THEMATIC ONLY** as a design principle, but it directly licenses the recommended recipe below: upgrade the renderer, keep the authored 2D art. **Staged combat camera** (push-in + reframe on big turns) → **STEAL cheaply**: on boss turns/critical hits, a short camera drift + vignette pulse in a turn-based game costs a tween, not a cutscene system. **Fog-sliced depth staging** (foreground silhouettes / midground / backplate) → **STEAL**: pure renderer dressing on a tile map (parallax dark tiles at map edge = the "map in a void" look). **Relationship/morale scenes given visual weight** (DD2's backstory/affinity staging) → **THEMATIC ONLY**: maps to NPC morale portraits/tinting, not staged scenes. |

## 3. Candidate visual targets for Laya Realms

### Target A — "Grim-lite 3D ortho" (Grim Dawn / PoE-1 recipe)

A full-3D low-poly world rendered through a fixed perspective camera at Grim Dawn pitch: chunky stylized terrain meshes and skinned low-poly actors, desaturated biome palettes with saturated gameplay accents, colored point lights at torches/spells, character "readability light" per actor, weather fog and per-biome color grading. AoE/loot readability over clutter is the Grim Dawn discipline.

- **Art pipeline required**: Blender low-poly kits (terrain blocks, props, buildings) + 3D actors with rigs and animations; palette/grading presets per biome. This *abandons* the current procedural 2D sprite art.
- **Bevy features required**: 3D PBR (standard), clustered point lights + shadows, distance fog / atmospheric scattering (Bevy 0.16), bloom, tone mapping, terrain as generated meshes. All commodity Bevy — no exotic rendering.
- **Content estimate (solo)**: the biggest of the three. Every actor species × animation set is 3D work the current codebase currently gets "free" in 2D; a credible biome kit is months of Blender, and it will still read "asset-flip indie" without an environment-art pass. Engine-side effort is low; art-side effort dominates everything.
- **Fog-of-war / turn mapping**: natural — per-tile visibility drives fog volumes and light intensity; night cycle drives atmosphere directional light; torch radius = a real shadow-casting point light parented to the explorer. Turn-based cadence tolerates heavy per-frame light counts.
- **Verdict**: technically easy, content-catastrophic for one dev, and it discards the one asset the project already has (authored procedural 2D art). Rejected as the primary target; mine it for palette/lighting discipline and the character-light-rig trick.

### Target B — "Painted iso à la Tyranny / Hades / Disco Elysium"

Hand-painted (or offline-rendered and overpainted) isometric environments with real-time actors on top: be it 3D→hand-painted characters (Hades), depth-passed pre-rendered backgrounds with matched light rigs (Tyranny), or painted-over 3D block-ins with normal/light-field passes (Disco Elysium).

- **Art pipeline required**: a painter, full-time, forever — plus an offline render/pass pipeline (Blender → depth/normal/albedo → paintover) and tools to register actors against a painted plane.
- **Bevy features required**: 2D/layered compositing, normal-mapped sprites (see sibling Bevy research), camera-projected textures, post stack. Moderate custom rendering, nothing impossible.
- **Content estimate (solo)**: the ceiling of all three and bounded by painting talent, not time. All three reference studios staffed professional environment painters; the look is purchasable only with that skill.
- **Fog-of-war / turn mapping**: poor structural fit — per-tile procedural map regeneration collides with per-scene hand-painted backgrounds. A roguelike's world is generated at runtime; the painted-iso recipe assumes authored scenes. Layering painted chunky *biome sets* is possible in principle but is a custom tile-compositing pipeline in itself.
- **Verdict**: the wrong fight for a solo procedural roguelike. Keep the Depth-of-Field-as-composition and "paint is the luxury" lessons; do not adopt the pipeline.

### Target C — "HD-2D pixel + lights à la Songs of Conquest / Octopath" ★ (recommended)

Keep the authored procedural pixel art (terrain, actors, silhouettes — the existing 32-unit pipeline), and spend the entire budget on a **3D lighting and post-processing layer wrapped around it**: a top-down projected light map rendered from the *game's own* torch light-radius / night-cycle / vision data (the Songs of Conquest system, but sourced from sim state), normal- or height-lit sprites so light pools wrap surfaces, bloom + depth of field + vignette + per-biome color grading for the photographic "diorama" frame, and restrained particles/point lights on spells and campfires à la Octopath combat lighting. Darkest Dungeon's thematic transfers layer directly on top: torch level drives global exposure/saturation decay, affliction/morale states get pose/tint tells, red-accent FX language, cheap staged camera pulses on boss turns.

- **Art pipeline required**: the current one. Optional upgrades: per-sprite height/normal maps (auto-generatable from silhouettes as a starting point), a handful of emissive variants (fire, magic), per-biome grading LUTs. No change to how tiles or actors are authored.
- **Bevy features required**: render-to-texture pass for the projected light map (sources the sim's per-tile light/vision array — cheap on any GPU, trivial on a 4090), normal-lit sprite material or custom sprite shader, bloom/DoF/vignette/grading post stack (Bevy built-ins; bloom shipped in 0.9, anamorphic controls in 0.16 — [bevy.org/news/bevy-0-16](https://bevy.org/news/bevy-0-16/)), optional fog volumes at map edges (the "map in a void" DD2-stage dressing). Feasibility details: sibling Bevy capability research in this directory.
- **Content estimate (solo)**: the lighting/post systems are weeks-to-a-couple-months of shader/compositing work, *one time*, and they raise every existing and future sprite. Art cost per new content stays roughly what it is today. This is the only recipe where engineering effort converts directly into permanent visual ceiling.
- **Fog-of-war / turn mapping**: this is where it stops being cosmetic and becomes the game's visual identity. The projected light texture **is** the fog-of-war: per-tile vision/light from the sim (torch radius decay, night cycle, revealed-but-unseen memory dimming) renders into the light map at tile resolution; unexplored tiles are black, explored-but-out-of-sight tiles get a desaturated "memory" tint, torch decay visibly drains warm light from the frame (the DD1 transfer). Turn-based cadence means the light map only recomputes on sim ticks — effectively free perf. Morale/stress states ride the same channel as colored pulse/tint overlays per actor.
- **Verdict**: recommended (below).

## 4. Recommendation

**Adopt Target C (Songs of Conquest recipe with an Octopath post stack), layered with Darkest Dungeon's thematic transfers.** It's the only reference in the set built by a comparably small team on procedurally generated maps — i.e. the only premium look whose content economics match a single dev: the sim's torch/night/vision state fed through a projected light map plus a bloom/DoF/grading stack buys "expensive depth" without adding a single new art asset, while A requires a 3D art department and B/C's painters are simply not hireable at this project's scale.
